How it was tested

Method and limits.

  • Data. A simulated network of 57 stations at 1-minute resolution: 9 cities, each with a main station and six neighbours, plus one remote station (Leh) with none. Weather has daily and multi-day cycles, station siting offsets, and genuine heatwaves, thunderstorms, cold fronts and fog.
  • Injected faults. Seven types (spike, sudden jump, slow drift, stuck value, noise burst, communication error, power glitch) at random times, sizes and stations. Half of the spikes, jumps, drifts and noise bursts are subtle.
  • Splits. The models learn from 8 clean days. The fusion stage learns from a separate 3-day set with injected faults. Scores come from three further 4-day sets that no model saw.
  • A fault counts as caught when an alert overlaps it on the same station. An alert with no overlapping fault is a false alarm. Time to alert runs from the fault's true start.
  • Limits. The data is simulated, so real IMD archives will be harder and should be used to retrain and re-score. Slow drift cannot be told from weather at a station with no neighbours. The engine expects 1-minute data.
